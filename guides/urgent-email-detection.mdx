Every message that lands in your Inbox is checked on arrival, and the ones that need attention today get an **Urgent** badge and a louder notification. Out of the box Mermail uses a sensible built-in definition of urgent. When that definition does not match how you work, you can replace it with your own rules, written in plain language.

This guide shows both: what the built-in behavior does, and how to write a custom rule. The example rule is one line — *emails from a specific client are always urgent* — and you will see it take effect on the next message that arrives.

## What you need

- A mailbox you can open in the [Mermail console](https://console.mermail.app).
- A few minutes to think about what "urgent" actually means for this mailbox. That is the real work here; typing the rule takes seconds.

## Open the setting

Click the gear icon beside the mailbox name to open **Settings**, then scroll to **Urgent email detection**.

The badge next to the section title tells you the current state at a glance:

| Badge | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Default** | Detection is on and uses Mermail's built-in rules. |
| **Custom rules** | Detection is on and uses only the rules you wrote. |
| **Off** | Nothing is marked urgent. No badges, no urgent alerts. |

## Start with the built-in rules

If you leave **Custom urgency rules** empty, Mermail applies its own definition. Click **View built-in default rules** to read them in full. In short, a message is marked urgent when it needs attention today — blocked customers, outages, payment failures, explicit deadlines, angry escalations, or time-sensitive requests from important contacts. Newsletters, FYI updates, marketing, routine notifications, "when you have a chance" requests, and automated reports are not urgent.

<Tip>
  Most mailboxes can leave the custom rules empty. Write your own only when the built-in definition misses something specific to your work, or flags things you do not care about.
</Tip>

## Write your own rules

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make sure detection is on">
    **Enable urgent email detection** must be toggled on. While it is off, the rules field is disabled and nothing is ever marked urgent.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Write the rules">
    Type your rules into **Custom urgency rules (optional)**, one rule per line. Say who or what is urgent, and be specific about the sender, domain, subject, or content that should match.

    ```text
    Emails from binhnnt.98@gmail.com are always urgent because he my client
    ```

    You can combine several rules, and you can state exceptions:

    ```text
    - Emails from @investor.com are always urgent
    - Payment failures and outage reports are urgent
    - Partnership intros are not urgent unless they mention a deadline
    ```

    The field holds up to 2,000 characters.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save Changes**. **Changes saved** appears beside the button and the badge at the top of the section switches to **Custom rules**. The next message that arrives is checked against your rules.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Custom rules **fully replace** the built-in defaults. Once you save a rule, outages and deadlines are no longer urgent unless your own rules say so. If you want the built-in behavior *plus* something extra, write out both.
</Warning>

## What happens when mail arrives

A message that matches your rules is flagged the moment it lands. In the inbox list it carries an **Urgent** badge alongside its other labels.

</Frame>

Open the message and the badge stays with the thread, next to its labels in the header.

<Frame caption="The Urgent badge follows the thread, so the flag is still visible while you read.">
  <img src="/images/guides/urgent-email-detection/urgent-thread.png" alt="Opened email thread titled Re: Invite you to design my app with an Urgent badge in the header" />

Urgent mail is also announced more loudly than ordinary mail: the in-app notification is styled as an alert rather than a quiet info message, and push and Telegram notifications are marked as urgent when you have them connected.

<Note>
  Only incoming Inbox mail is checked. Messages you send, and mail that never reaches the Inbox, are never marked urgent.
</Note>

## How Mermail decides

Knowing the decision rules makes it much easier to predict the result:

- **A rule that names a sender or domain applies only to that sender or domain.** "Emails from `alice@acme.com` are urgent" never makes `bob@acme.com` urgent.
- **No clear match means not urgent.** Mermail does not stretch a rule to cover something similar.
- **Ambiguity resolves to not urgent.** Vague wording such as "important stuff is urgent" will mostly produce nothing at all.
- **Your rules are the only criteria.** With custom rules saved, Mermail deliberately ignores general email-triage instincts such as outages, deadlines, or angry tone unless you named them.

## Write rules that work

- **Name the thing that matches** — a sender address, a domain, a subject phrase, or specific content — instead of describing a feeling.
- **Say what is *not* urgent** too. Exceptions are what keep the badge meaningful.
- **Avoid "all", "every", and "always urgent" on their own.** If your rules look like they will flag most of your mail, Mermail asks you to confirm before saving, because badges and alerts stop being useful when everything carries them.
- **Review after a week.** If nothing was flagged, your rules are probably too narrow or too vague. If everything was flagged, they are too broad.

<Tip>
  The guiding line is printed right under the toggle: be specific — if everything is urgent, nothing is.
</Tip>

## Turn it off or start over

- **Reset to default** appears in the section header once you have custom rules. It clears the rules field and puts the mailbox back on the built-in defaults. Save afterwards to apply it.
- **Toggle off** stops detection entirely. No message is marked urgent, no badges appear, and no urgent alerts are sent. Your saved rules are kept, so you can switch it back on later.
